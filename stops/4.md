### Bamum royal tunic  

> Cameroon, 20th century  

> The Textile Museum Collection 2007.30.3

Status is shown through garments in every culture. This tunic from the Bamiléké and Bamum
kingdoms of Cameroon would have been worn for ceremonies and dances by a member of the
royal court in service of the divine chief, called a fon. The garment’s prestigious history is
evident through its impressive craftsmanship, the additions of animal hair and expensive red
fabric, as well as the motifs.

The diamond designs held royal significance, while the large circles
represent kola nuts—a symbol of hospitality. The designs were made with a stitch-resist dye
technique. Stitch resist is when a pattern is sewn into a textile before dyeing, in this case with
raffia grass. After dyeing, the stitches are removed to reveal the pattern.
