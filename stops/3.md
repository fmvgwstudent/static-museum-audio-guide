## Rosa Ordoñez (weaver)

### Huipil

### Guatemala, c. 1988-1998

### The Textile Museum Collection 1999.26.2


While textiles are often used as tools by the elite to display status, any garment can tell stories
about their wearers’ identities and their communities. A prominent example of this is huipiles, a
typical women’s garment among indigenous communities in Central America and Mexico,
particularly in Maya communities. These weavers developed distinctive styles that have evolved
overtime, reflecting the availability of new materials and cultural influences. This huipil was
woven by Rosa Ordoñez in the late 20th century in Santa Catarina Palopó, a Mayan community
on the shores of Lake Atitlán. This community originally featured primarily red and white colors
in their huipil designs, but now they are famous for blue. You can see how this huipil was made
during the transitional period of these styles because of its use of both color schemes.
