## Rosa Ordoñez (weaver)

### Huipil

> Guatemala, c. 1988-1998

> The Textile Museum Collection 1999.26.2


Textiles are often used as tools by the elite to display status, but any garment can tell us stories about its wearer
and the communities that they come from. Huipiles are a great example of that. Huipiles are typical garments worn by indigenous women in communities of Mexico
and Central America, particularly in Maya communities. Different communities have different techniques and styles they use in their huipiles and these styles will change over time
as communities are introduced to new materials and new cultural influences.

This huipil was woven by Rosa Ordonez in the late 20th century in the community
of Santa Catarina Palopó, a Maya community on the shores of Lake Atitlán. This community was originally known for red
and white weaving styles in their huipiles, but now they are famous for blue. We can tell that this huipil was woven in the transitional
period between these two styles because of its use of both colorways.
