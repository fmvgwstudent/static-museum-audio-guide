### Furisode and replacement sleeves   

> Japan, c. 1930s   

> The Textile Museum Collection 2010.11.1A-C

 
Variations in kimono design can also tell us stories
of their wearers identity.

For example, sleeve length in kimono represents marital status. Long sleeved kimono, such as this one called furisode,
are meant for unmarried women, and so the last time a woman would wear a furisode is on the day of her wedding.
This kimono was made with a pair of matching shorter sleeves called kaesode. This way, the long sleeves could be swapped
for the shorter sleeves following the wedding, so the woman could continue to wear the kimono into her married life.

This kimono also features pine tree designs, which are auspicious symbols often used to adorn garments for formal and ceremonial occasions, such as weddings
