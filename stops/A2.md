### Kazakh man’s robe   

> Uzbekistan, 1850-1900  

> The Textile Museum Collection 2002.5.1 


Many pieces in <em>Enduring Traditions</em> represent the peak of textile artisanship and their time and place.
These items would've been owned by people of high status, and they represent their power and their prestige. This robe is a supreme example of that.

This robe was likely made by an Uzbek tailor for a Kazakh man of status. Possibly even a Khan. Khans were tribal leaders
and the Khan who may have owned this robe would've saved it for the most important occasions such as weddings
or horseback riding competitions.

This robe is made of doe skin leather and is decorated with very fine embroidery featuring motifs from three
different regions of Asia. The round design is reminiscent of Chinese Tibetan art, whereas the oblong designs harken to the wider Eurasian steppe.
And finally, the border designs were popular motifs in domestic textiles in Western Central Asia in the late 19th century, which is when this robe was made.
