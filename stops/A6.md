### Ritual cloth (pua kumbu)   

> Malaysia, 19th century  

> The Textile Museum Collection 2000.22.8  


<em>Enduring Traditions</em> also feature objects that tell us stories of community, spirituality, and religious practices. This cloth called a pua is from the Iban people
of the island of Borneo, and these cloths would be used to define ritual space, cover ritual food, or assist shamans in other spiritual practices.
They're woven with designs meant to invoke ancestors or gods. This pua features large serpent designs. Serpents were one of the most important symbols
to the Iban people because serpents would guard the entrance to the underworld and also featured prominently in creation myths.

Something that is unique about this pua is it is missing its lateral borders. Pua designs are typically woven with lateral borders
because the borders were supposed to help contain the dangerous power of the spirits they were invoking. It is likely that this pua included the lateral borders
originally in its creation, but they became separated at some point in its history.
