## Kazakh man’s robe   

 Uzbekistan, 1850-1900  

 The Textile Museum Collection 2002.5.1 


Many pieces in Enduring Traditions represent the peak of textile artisanship in their time and
place. These items belonged to people with great power, wealth, and status in their
communities. This man’s robe is a supreme example of that. It was likely made by an Uzbek
tailor for a Kazakh man of status such as a khan. Khans were tribal leaders, and the khan who
may have owned this robe would have reserved it for important celebrations and special
occasions such as weddings and horseback riding competitions. The exterior of the robe is
made of a finely embroidered leather and features motifs drawn from three different regions of
Asia: the central round design resembles Chinese-Tibetan art, the oblong design harkens to the
wider Eurasian Steppe, and the border designs were popular to West Central Asia in the late
nineteenth century, which is when this robe was made.
