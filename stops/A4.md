### Bamum royal tunic  

> Cameroon, 20th century  

> The Textile Museum Collection 2007.30.3


Prestige is shown through garments in every culture. This tunic from the Bamileke and Bamum kingdoms of Cameroon would've been warned
for ceremonial dances in the high court of their divine chief called a Fon. We can tell the prestige of this tunic due to a multitude
of factors, the quality of its craftsmanship, its use of animal hair, and bright red, expensive fabric as well as its motifs.

The diamond designs represent the royal family, and the circular designs represent Kola nuts, which are a symbol of hospitality.
The design in this tunic was made with a stitch resist dye technique. Stitch resist is when the design is sewn into the
textile before dyeing. In this case, the textile was sewn with raffia grass and died in indigo. After dyeing, the stitches are removed to reveal the pattern.
