### Ritual cloth (pua kumbu)   

> Malaysia, 19th century  

> The Textile Museum Collection 2000.22.8  

<em>Enduring Traditions</em> also features textiles that tell us stories of communities’ spirituality and
religious practices. This cloth, called a pua, comes from the Iban people of the island of Borneo.
Pua would be displayed during ceremonies and special occasions to invoke ancestors and gods.
They would be used to define ritual space, cover ritual food, or assist the shaman in other ways.
This pua depicts large serpents, which are one of the most powerful Iban images, because they
were thought to guard the door to the land of the dead. They also appear in creation stories.

What is unusual in this example is it does not include lateral borders. Borders are included in
pua designs because they are believed to help contain the potentially dangerous power of the
spirits they invoke. It is likely that this textile did include the borders at one time, but they
became separated at some point in its history.
