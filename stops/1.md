Welcome to the George Washington University Museum and The Textile Museum. My name is
Haley Shattuck, and I am the Programs Associate for Public Engagement at the museum. Since
its founding in 1925, the museum’s collection has become one of the world’s most significant
collections of handmade textile art, with more than 21,000 works representing five continents
and five millennia. Today I’ll be taking you on a tour of our second centennial exhibition
<em>Enduring Traditions: Celebrating the World of Textiles</em>.

<em>Enduring Traditions</em> features a diverse array of textile masterworks created over the last
thousand years. The exhibit focuses on the cultural significance of these pieces and highlights
important stories of the people who made and used them. Together we will explore several
objects on view in this exhibit that showcase how textiles represented power, spirituality, and
identity in communities across the world, as well as the significance they hold for descendent
communities today.
