### Furisode and replacement sleeves   

> Japan, c. 1930s   

> The Textile Museum Collection 2010.11.1A-C

Variations in design in kimono can also tell us a lot about its wearer and their identity, as well as
what occasion it was worn for and during which season of the year.

For example, the sleeve length of a kimono indicates marital status. Long sleeved kimonos, such as this one, called
furisode, were traditionally worn by unmarried women. Thus, the last time a woman would
wear a furisode is during her wedding. We can see that this furisode is accompanied by a pair of
matching shorter sleeves, called kaesode, which would replace the longer sleeves so the bride
could continue to wear the kimono long into her married life.

This kimono also features pine
tree designs which are auspicious symbols often used to adorn garments for formal or
ceremonial occasions, such as weddings.
